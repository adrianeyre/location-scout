## Using the public APIs responsibly

Phase 1 relies on free public services, so please keep usage light:

- Nominatim allows at most **1 request per second** and needs an identifying User-Agent. The backend queues address searches so it never sends more than that, and caches each one for 7 days. Searches run only when you press Search, never as you type.
- data.police.uk allows 15 requests a second. The backend sends at most **10 a second** and caches each month's crimes for 24 hours.
- planning.data.gov.uk publishes no rate limit. A new Search Area makes two requests, plus one for each further page of 500 constraints, and each answer is cached for 24 hours.
- Overpass is for fair use: the public instance gives each IP address a couple of query slots at once. Every Layer from OpenStreetMap shares **one query per Search Area** (Amenities, Transport and Green space so far), queries for different Search Areas go at least a second apart, each asks for a quarter of the memory Overpass would give it by default and looks only near the Location, and each answer is cached for 24 hours.
- HM Land Registry publishes no rate limit for Price Paid's SPARQL endpoint. A new Search Area makes one request to postcodes.io for its postcodes, cached for 7 days. It then makes one query to Price Paid for all of their sales in the last 12 months, and once that answers, three more together: one for every sale at those sales' addresses, and one each for the 12 months a year and five years before. In a Land Coverage Area, the Land Layer makes one more, for the land sales near its Land Opportunities, as each change of the Land panel's filters in the Search Area and each Land Opportunity chosen does: a query for the 494 postcodes within a mile of Sutton Parkway's farm field answered in a quarter of a second on 9 October 2026, and one for 1,017 postcodes did too. Each is cached for 24 hours.
- `pnpm data:prepare` downloads the council boundaries from ONS Open Geography once a run, about 19 MB, and the Demographics Layer's datasets: the output area boundaries and their lookup from ONS Open Geography, about 270 requests made one after another, Census 2021's four tables from Nomis, ONS's income estimates and the Indices of Deprivation, one request each. It downloads GIAS's schools register in four requests, its Downloads page, its form, the page saying the file is ready (asked again every 2 seconds while GIAS makes it) and the zip, and Ofsted's latest inspections in two, gov.uk's Content API for the page and the CSV. It downloads Ordnance Survey's Code-Point Open once a run, 14 MB, through the OS Downloads API's redirect. For the Land Coverage Areas it asks HM Land Registry's INSPIRE download page twice (once for its cookies) and then each council's zip, one after another; Geofabrik's index and each county extract it needs once a run, the smallest that do, rather than England's 1.7 GB; and planning.data.gov.uk's six Parquet files once a run, 613 MB, however many councils it prepares. Each says it is Location Scout's `data:prepare`. Nothing is downloaded while the app runs.
- PlanIt allows about **1 request per minute**, so every request to it waits its turn in a queue, a minute after the one before, and the first search of a new area may wait for its Planning Applications while the rest loads. A `429 Too Many Requests` holds every request back until its `Retry-After` has passed. Each request identifies us with `PLANIT_USER_AGENT`, names no day, so it is the same all week, and is cached for 7 days, so a Search Area asks PlanIt once a week ([Planning Applications](layers-and-street-view.md#layers)).
- MHCLG's Energy Performance of Buildings Data API, with `EPC_API_KEY`, allows **6,000 requests every 5 minutes** per IP address and asks for requests a few at a time rather than in batches. A new Search Area makes one search for each of its postcodes, at most 100, and one request for each sale matched, at most 6 at once: NG17 1AA's makes 119. Each answer is cached for 24 hours, and after a `429 Too Many Requests` nothing more is asked until its `Retry-After`, or a minute.
- Google's Street View, with `GOOGLE_MAPS_API_KEY`, is loaded only when you open the panel ([Street View](layers-and-street-view.md#street-view)). Nothing is loaded from Google without the key.
- Anthropic's API, with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, is asked only when you choose a question, once for each question about the same figures, which are kept in memory for 24 hours, and each request is made with your key ([AI Explanations](ai-explanations.md#ai-explanations)). `pnpm test:ai` asks it 19 questions. Nothing calls Anthropic without the key.
- PlanWire, when it is the provider, allows a number of requests a day (25 with a free key), resetting at midnight UTC. Each request names no day and is cached for 7 days, so a Search Area asks it once a week, and once it answers `429 Too Many Requests` it is asked nothing more until its `Retry-After`, or midnight UTC ([PlanWire](layers-and-street-view.md#planwire)).
- `pnpm smoke` calls each live Data Source once, one after another, about 14 requests with every key set, and only when you run it ([Smoke script](testing.md#smoke-script)). It sends PlanIt one request, so run it at most once a minute, and a free PlanWire key one of its 25 a day.
- The OpenStreetMap tile servers and the public Overpass instance are for light use only. They are not for production or commercial hosting, so Phase 2 replaces them.
- Police crime locations are **deliberately approximate**.
- **Land Opportunities warrant further investigation.** They say nothing about ownership or availability. Unregistered land still has an owner.

## Attribution

Every Data Source's licence asks for its data to be credited wherever it is shown (PRD §6.15). The credits are written once, in `apps/web/src/attribution.ts`, and every view gives them from there:

- **The footer** of every view gives PRD FR-10.1's credit for the sources almost every Search Area draws on: "© [OpenStreetMap contributors](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) ([ODbL](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/)). police.uk, HM Land Registry and planning.data.gov.uk data under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/). Contains OS data © Crown copyright and database right." Its **Data sources and licences** button opens a dialog listing every source below, what it is used for and its credit, with "[year]" where the year is that of the data wherever it is shown.
- **The map** credits its tiles to OpenStreetMap's contributors in its corner, always expanded (FR-10.4).
- **Each Feature's popup** ends with the credits of the Data Sources its Features come from, each once, with their links (`featureCredits` in `attribution.ts`).
- **The Location Overview** credits the Planning Constraints, Planning Applications, Schools, Property and Demographics Layers' sources beneath their statistics, and the Land panel and Land Coverage Area the land data's, dated. Crime and the OpenStreetMap Layers are credited by the footer beside them.
- **The printable report** gives the footer's credit and then the Location's and every Layer's, as their Data Sources answered ([the report](saved-locations-compare-and-report.md#printable-report)).

Each source's credit, as the app gives it, then where it appears. A year or date in brackets is that of the data: the app fills it in wherever it knows it. `apps/web/src/attribution.test.ts` checks that every credit here is the app's, word for word, with the same links, and that this README's footer credit is too.

- **OpenStreetMap** (the base map, Nominatim, the Overpass API and Geofabrik's extracts): Map data © [OpenStreetMap contributors](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright), [ODbL](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/). OpenStreetMap's [copyright page](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) asks for its contributors to be credited and its licence named and linked. A popup of the Amenities, Transport and Green space Layers adds "from the Overpass API", the report its date, and the Land Layer "from Geofabrik's extracts, which Land Parcels are built from".
- **data.police.uk:** Crime data from [police.uk](https://data.police.uk/), licensed under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/). Police crime locations are deliberately approximate, and the app says so.
- **planning.data.gov.uk:** Planning Constraints from [planning.data.gov.uk](https://www.planning.data.gov.uk/), licensed under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/). Each of its datasets names its own attribution: listed building outlines, Article 4 direction areas, tree preservation zones and flood risk zones "© Crown copyright and database right", which the footer's credit gives, and conservation areas and listed buildings Historic England's, below. The Land Layer credits its bulk files.
- **Historic England**, for planning.data.gov.uk's conservation areas and listed buildings: Conservation areas and listed buildings: © Historic England [year]. Contains Ordnance Survey data © Crown copyright and database right [year]. The Historic England GIS Data contained in this material was obtained on [date]. The most publicly available up to date Historic England GIS Data can be obtained from [HistoricEngland.org.uk](https://historicengland.org.uk). That is the statement planning.data.gov.uk gives both datasets (`attribution-text` in [`conservation-area.json`](https://www.planning.data.gov.uk/dataset/conservation-area.json) and [`listed-building.json`](https://www.planning.data.gov.uk/dataset/listed-building.json), 9 October 2026). The date is the day the backend obtained the Planning Constraints, which `/api/area/planning` gives as planning.data.gov.uk's `period` and keeps while they are cached, or the day `pnpm data:prepare` dates the bulk files by.
- **PlanIt**, by default: Planning Applications from [PlanIt](https://www.planit.org.uk/). Locations PlanIt looks up from postcodes contain OS data © Crown copyright and database right, Royal Mail data © Royal Mail copyright and database right, and National Statistics data © Crown copyright and database right, under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/). The Location Overview and report add the day PlanIt had them on. PlanIt's [FAQ](https://www.planit.org.uk/faq/) says of its data "You can use the data for anything you want. No attribution is necessary", and asks for a User-Agent with a contact email, one request a minute at most, honouring `429` and its `Retry-After`, and, for bulk collection, about 300 requests a day. Its terms: "there are no commercial terms"; it is a non-profit, run on a best-efforts basis with no guaranteed service, and "it would not be wise to base any commercial services on the API", which is why PlanWire is the intended provider once the product is hosted (ADR 0003, PRD Phase 2). PlanIt only links to councils' planning documents, which are copyright of the applicant, agent or architect and may be used only for consultation and comparison (its [acknowledgements](https://www.planit.org.uk/acknowledgements/)); the app links to an application's page on the council's website and on PlanIt, and copies no document.
- **PlanWire**, when it is the provider: Planning Applications from [PlanWire](https://planwire.io/). Its [terms](https://planwire.io/terms) (version of 24 September 2026) say the planning data it serves is public sector information under the Open Government Licence v3.0 where applicable, while its compilation is PlanWire's; they forbid misrepresenting the source of its data to end users and redistributing its raw feed as a dataset or API of its own; and they let you display its data in your own product, and cache and store its responses while your access lasts. They ask for no attribution wording. Whether that settles OQ-1 for Phase 2 is for a terms review, not this guide.
- **HM Land Registry Price Paid Data** (sales, sale histories and land sales): Contains HM Land Registry data © Crown copyright and database right [year]. This data is licensed under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/). Price Paid Data contains address data processed against Ordnance Survey’s AddressBase Premium product, which incorporates Royal Mail’s PAF® database (Address Data). Royal Mail and Ordnance Survey permit its use for personal and/or non-commercial use, and to display for the purpose of providing residential property price information services. That is HM Land Registry's required statement and its address-data conditions, from its [Price Paid Data page](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/price-paid-data-downloads) (FR-10.3); the year is that of the latest sale asked for.
- **HM Land Registry INSPIRE Index Polygons:** INSPIRE Index Polygons: This information is subject to Crown copyright and database rights [year] and is reproduced with the permission of HM Land Registry. The polygons (including the associated geometry, namely x, y co-ordinates) are subject to Crown copyright and database rights [year] Ordnance Survey AC0000851063. [Conditions of use](https://use-land-property-data.service.gov.uk/datasets/inspire/#conditions). The year is the one the polygons were published, which `pnpm data:prepare` stores with them (FR-10.2).
- **HM Land Registry CCOD and OCOD:** UK and overseas companies that own property in England and Wales (CCOD and OCOD): Information produced by HM Land Registry. © Crown copyright [year]. [CCOD licence](https://use-land-property-data.service.gov.uk/datasets/ccod/licence/view), [OCOD licence](https://use-land-property-data.service.gov.uk/datasets/ocod/licence/view). The year and date are those HM Land Registry published them, which the Location Overview, the Land panel and each popup that lists a title give.
- **Energy Performance Certificates**, with `EPC_API_KEY`: Energy Performance Certificate data from MHCLG's [Get energy performance of buildings data](https://get-energy-performance-data.communities.gov.uk/), licensed under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/). The certificates' addresses and postcodes are Ordnance Survey's and Royal Mail's, permitted only for energy-efficiency purposes, so they are used only to match sales and never shown, and need no notice ([OQ-8](../../docs/spikes/oq-8-epc-api.md#licence)).
- **postcodes.io** (postcodes, local geography and the postcodes in a Search Area): Postcode data via [postcodes.io](https://postcodes.io): Contains OS data © Crown copyright and database right. Contains Royal Mail data © Royal Mail copyright and database right. Source: Office for National Statistics licensed under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/). postcodes.io serves the ONS Postcode Directory, whose [licence](https://www.ons.gov.uk/methodology/geography/licences) asks for those three statements. postcodes.io doesn't say which release it serves, so they name no year.
- **Ordnance Survey Code-Point Open**, which places land sales and titles: Code-Point Open: Contains OS data © Crown copyright and database right [year]. Contains Royal Mail data © Royal Mail copyright and database right [year]. Contains National Statistics data © Crown copyright and database right [year]. [Conditions of use](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/). The year is that of its release, which `pnpm data:prepare` stores with each Land Coverage Area's postcodes.
- **ONS Open Geography's council boundaries:** Council boundaries: Source: Office for National Statistics licensed under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/). Contains OS data © Crown copyright and database right [year].
- **ONS Census 2021, output area boundaries, small area income estimates and the English Indices of Deprivation:** Census, boundary and income data: Source: Office for National Statistics licensed under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/). Contains OS data © Crown copyright and database right 2021. English Indices of Deprivation © Crown copyright, Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government, licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0.
- **Get Information about Schools and Ofsted:** Contains public sector information from the Department for Education's Get Information about Schools and Ofsted, licensed under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/).
- **Google Street View**, with `GOOGLE_MAPS_API_KEY`: Street View imagery: Google Maps. [Terms](https://www.google.com/intl/en-GB_ALL/help/terms_maps/). Google's panorama carries Google's own logo, copyright and terms, which are never covered, and the Street View panel credits it beneath with a link to Google's terms, as Google's [attribution guidelines](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/javascript/policies) ask: "Google Maps" as written, never translated.
- **Anthropic** writes AI Explanations from figures the app has already credited, and supplies no data of its own, so it has no credit; every explanation is labelled "AI Explanation, based only on the figures above" ([AI Explanations](ai-explanations.md)).
