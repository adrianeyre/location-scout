# Testing

Unit, AI and end-to-end tests, the live smoke script, the benchmark and the survey of 20 Locations. Back to the [README](../../README.md).

### Test

```bash
pnpm test          # unit tests (no network calls)
pnpm test:e2e      # Playwright end-to-end test against a stubbed backend
pnpm test:ai       # opt-in: asks ANTHROPIC_MODEL about recorded inputs; fails on any number not in its input
pnpm smoke         # opt-in: calls each live Data Source once; fails if one no longer answers as the app expects
pnpm bench         # opt-in: times a Search Area and a Land Opportunity search against PRD NFR-2.1 to NFR-2.5
pnpm survey        # opt-in: loads 20 real Locations in every Scout Mode, for tuning the scores by
```

`pnpm test` reads no `data/`: tests that need prepared data read the tiny fixture in `apps/api/src/testing/prepared-data/`, and the rest run as if nothing were prepared. `scripts/data-prepare` tests `pnpm data:prepare` against recordings, writing into a temporary folder: three councils' boundaries (`scripts/data-prepare/src/fixtures/ons-council-boundaries.json`: Ashfield, Mansfield and Cardiff, which is left out as Welsh), and small parts of the Demographics Layer's sources, recorded around NG17 1AA and Sutton Parkway (`recordedSources` in `scripts/data-prepare/src/recorded-sources.ts`): the 92 output areas reaching into a box around both, their 22 LSOAs and 7 MSOAs, and those rows of each Census table, the income workbook and the Indices of Deprivation, with their headers and layout as they are, and a Welsh row in each, which is left out. And small parts of the Schools Layer's, recorded on 9 October 2026: GIAS's Downloads form and the page giving its file, and its zip with the 201 establishments within about 6.5 km of NG17 1AA (93 open), the two independent schools nearest it, and a few elsewhere for what they test (a school whose name needs Windows-1252 decoding, one GIAS knows no council for, a Welsh nursery), with headteachers' and proprietors' names blanked; gov.uk's Content API for Ofsted's page, with four of its attachments; and Ofsted's CSV of latest inspections as at 31 August 2026, with those schools' rows. And small parts of each Land Coverage Area's land data, recorded on 9 October 2026 (`record-land-fixtures.ts`): HM Land Registry's INSPIRE download page, and Ashfield's and Mansfield's zips with only the polygons in three small boxes, around Sutton Parkway's 0.25 mile Search Area, where Ashfield meets Mansfield, and near Pinxton, where Geofabrik's outlines of Nottinghamshire and Derbyshire overlap; Geofabrik's index, with England and those two counties, and their extracts with only the ways in those boxes, and Hartshorne Wood, a multipolygon no area can be built from, written as `.osm.pbf` by the recorder; a few rows of each of planning.data.gov.uk's six datasets, including a listed building in Mansfield whose listing ended in 2023, and one far away in each; and ONS's names for all 296 English councils, so `inspire.test.ts` checks that each finds its own INSPIRE file. From them `pnpm data:prepare` builds five Land Parcels in Ashfield and one in Mansfield, which `prepare.test.ts` checks are inside each boundary, clear of buildings and roads, and tagged as the rules say. `land-parcels.test.ts` checks each of the rules on a made-up council a kilometre square, as small as they need: open land joined where it overlaps and parted by a road, a house, a stream and a park taken away while a footpath isn't, a sliver too small, land across the council's boundary, a title covering one parcel and a quarter of another, a conservation area, a listed building, its outline, flood zones 2 and 3, a road too far away, and the capacity of each. To record them again, download those sources into a folder, as `record-land-fixtures.ts` lists, and run `pnpm --filter @location-scout/data-prepare fixture:record-land <folder>`. Both need DuckDB's spatial extension, which `pnpm install` installs. CCOD and OCOD can't be recorded without an account, so `land-registry-ccod.zip` and `land-registry-ocod.zip` are **made up**, in HM Land Registry's published layout: titles numbered ZZ…, which HM Land Registry doesn't use, with placeholder proprietors, whose addresses name buildings the recorded extracts map, so three match one polygon each, one (Kings Mill Hospital) names a building over two and matches none, and the rest are leasehold, land described by its street, in Mansfield, in Harrogate (no English council's name now) or in Wales. HM Land Registry's API is stubbed as its documentation says it answers, to `RECORDED_LAND_REGISTRY_API_KEY`. Code-Point Open is recorded with the postcodes within 2.5 km of the recorded boxes (`codepo_gb.zip`, 2,860 postcodes of its August 2026 release, downloaded on 9 October 2026, `recordCodePointOpen` in `record-land-fixtures.ts`): every postcode within a mile of the Land Parcels built from the recordings, as the whole of Code-Point Open gives them, and those the titles give. The API's prepared-data fixture is made with that key, so it has Ownership Categories: Sutton Parkway's farm field is other registered, with an overseas company's made-up title matched to Metal Craft Group's polygon 9 m away. `prepare.test.ts` checks the download, the titles kept, the matches and the warnings without a key, with a refused key, without Code-Point Open and with a CSV whose columns have changed, and `land-parcels.test.ts` checks each category, the titles listed within 25 m, and that without CCOD and OCOD there are none. `prepare.test.ts` also checks that each council keeps the postcodes within a mile of it, across its boundary too, and none beyond, and that without Code-Point Open the rest is prepared, with a warning. `land-sales.test.ts` checks the postcodes found within a mile of Sutton Parkway's farm field, the land sales placed at them, each Land Opportunity's own and all of them as far as the nearest, the newest 10 listed, and that they are unavailable without Price Paid and not prepared without the postcodes, asking Price Paid nothing. `nearby-applications.test.ts` checks which Planning Applications count as a Land Opportunity's nearby development activity, on a made-up square of land 100 m across, and `app.test.ts` checks the Land Layer's at Sutton Parkway against a made-up PlanIt answer for its Search Area, numbered ZZ…, while it is queued and when PlanIt fails, and that PlanIt isn't asked across a whole Land Coverage Area. It also checks that the Planning Layer and the scores asking at once send PlanIt one request, that the scores say Development activity and Demand are queued while PlanIt's turn is taken, and that the Highlights ask PlanIt nothing.

The end-to-end tests drive Chromium, which Playwright downloads separately. Install it once, and again whenever `@playwright/test` is upgraded:

```bash
pnpm --filter @location-scout/web exec playwright install --with-deps chromium
```

`--with-deps` installs the browser's system libraries too, which on Linux needs `sudo`. Leave it off if you already have them.

## End-to-end tests

`pnpm test:e2e` runs the Playwright tests in `apps/web/e2e/` (PRD NFR-3.2). It starts two servers of its own, so it doesn't matter whether `pnpm dev` is running:

- **The API on port 8797, with recorded fixtures for every Data Source** (`pnpm --filter @location-scout/api start:fixtures`). It is the real app, but its `fetch` is `recordedFetch` from `apps/api/src/testing/fixture-fetch.ts`. That answers each call from a fixture and refuses, without sending it, any call it has no fixture for, so that Layer reads as unavailable. Nothing reaches a Data Source (NFR-3.3).
- **The frontend on port 5183**, whose `/api` proxy points at that API.
- **A third API on port 8799, and a frontend on port 5185 pointing at it**, started with `PLANNING_APPLICATIONS_PROVIDER=planwire` and the key the stubbed PlanWire answers to, for the tests in `*.planwire.spec.ts`.
- **A fourth API on port 8800, and a frontend on port 5186 pointing at it**, started with `EPC_API_KEY` set to the token the stubbed EPC API answers to, for the tests in `*.epc.spec.ts`. Every other API runs without one, as the app does unless it is set, so `property-epc.spec.ts` checks nothing about EPCs is shown without it.
- **A fifth API on port 8801, and a frontend on port 5187 pointing at it**, started with `GOOGLE_MAPS_API_KEY`, for the Street View tests in `*.google-maps.spec.ts`. They stub Google's script in the browser (`page.route`), so it answers as Google's would without reaching Google: a panorama 20 m south of wherever it is asked. Every other API runs without a key, so `street-view.spec.ts` checks that no Street View is offered and no Google script is loaded without it.
- **A sixth API on port 8802, and a frontend on port 5188 pointing at it**, started with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, for the AI Explanation tests in `*.anthropic.spec.ts`. Its model is a stub (`stubAnthropic` in `apps/api/src/testing/anthropic-stub.ts`, which `recordedFetch` answers Anthropic's Messages API with): it streams, as Anthropic does, an answer saying what it was asked, in three parts 1.5 s apart, so the tests see it streaming even on a busy machine, adds FR-5.7's wording when its instructions ask for it, and, asked "How does this compare with the national average?", makes up 987,654. ## Smoke script

No test reaches a Data Source (PRD NFR-3.3), so nothing above notices when one changes its answers, moves or goes away. `pnpm smoke` does: it calls each live Data Source once, with the request the app makes, and maps the answer with the adapter's own code into our types, about NG17 1AA's 0.5 mile Search Area, which the recordings cover too. It is opt-in, and never run in CI or by `pnpm test`, because it reaches the real services. Run it before recording fixtures again, or when a Layer reads "Unavailable" and you want to know which source changed:

```bash
pnpm smoke
```

It reads `.env`, as the API does, and says for each source whether it passed, failed or was skipped, with how many requests it sent and how long they took. Each call has the app's 8-second timeout, and Anthropic's its 60 seconds. It exits with `1` if any source failed, and `0` otherwise, skipped ones included:

```text
PASS  postcodes.io: NG17 1AA is in Sutton Central & New Cross, Ashfield, England (1 request, 74 ms)
SKIP  Nominatim: no NOMINATIM_USER_AGENT, which it asks every request to identify us with
PASS  data.police.uk: 185 crimes around NG17 1AA in 2026-08, the latest month published (2 requests, 261 ms)
PASS  planning.data.gov.uk: 9 Planning Constraints around NG17 1AA, 1 covering it (2 requests, 132 ms)
SKIP  Overpass API: no NOMINATIM_USER_AGENT, which it asks every request to identify us with
PASS  HM Land Registry Price Paid: 24 sales in the last 12 months in 100 postcodes around NG17 1AA (2 requests, 99 ms)
SKIP  PlanIt: no PLANIT_USER_AGENT or NOMINATIM_USER_AGENT, one of which it asks for
SKIP  PlanWire: no PLANWIRE_API_KEY
SKIP  Energy Performance of Buildings Data API: no EPC_API_KEY
SKIP  Anthropic API: no ANTHROPIC_API_KEY

Google Street View isn't checked: the browser loads it with GOOGLE_MAPS_API_KEY, which only works from the frontend's origin, and the API never calls Google. Open the Street View panel to check it.

4 passed, 0 failed, 6 skipped.
```

That was a run on 9 October 2026 with nothing set. A source fails if it can't be reached, doesn't answer within 8 seconds, refuses its key (saying which setting to check), answers `429 Too Many Requests`, or gives an answer the adapter can't map. It fails too if the answer maps but has lost what it has always had, such as a place for the address or a crime in the month, as when a field is renamed and the adapter quietly leaves out every record. What each check asks, in order:

| Data Source | What it asks | Requests | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| postcodes.io | NG17 1AA, which must be in England with its ward and local authority | 1 | |
| Nominatim | "12 High Street, Sutton-in-Ashfield", which must find a place | 1 | `NOMINATIM_USER_AGENT` |
| data.police.uk | The latest month published, then the crimes in it, of which there must be some | 2 | |
| planning.data.gov.uk | The Planning Constraints, and the listed building outlines at the Location, at least one of which must cover it, as Sutton-in-Ashfield's conservation area does | 2, and 1 more for each further page of 500 | |
| Overpass API | The one query every Layer from OpenStreetMap shares, which must find elements and date its copy of OpenStreetMap | 1 | `NOMINATIM_USER_AGENT` |
| HM Land Registry Price Paid | postcodes.io for the Search Area's postcodes, then Price Paid for their sales in the last 12 months, of which there must be some | 2 | |
| PlanIt | The Planning Applications of the last 3 years, of which there must be some | 1 | `PLANIT_USER_AGENT` or `NOMINATIM_USER_AGENT` |
| PlanWire | The same, from PlanWire, whatever `PLANNING_APPLICATIONS_PROVIDER` says | 1 | `PLANWIRE_API_KEY` |
| Energy Performance of Buildings Data API | NG17 1EJ's certificates, then the first one's, which must give a floor area. It says only their bands and floor area, never an address, which the EPC licence allows only for energy-efficiency purposes | 2 | `EPC_API_KEY` |
| Anthropic API | `ANTHROPIC_MODEL`, asked to reply "OK": a few dozen tokens | 1 | `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` |

A source without what it needs is skipped and sent nothing. Nominatim and Overpass refuse requests that don't say who sends them, so without `NOMINATIM_USER_AGENT` the script skips them rather than failing, unlike the API, which won't start. The API never calls Google, so Street View isn't checked; open its panel to check your key. The prepared Layers' sources (Census, GIAS, INSPIRE and the rest) are downloaded by `pnpm data:prepare`, not called while the app runs, so they aren't checked either; running `pnpm data:prepare` checks them.

The checks keep within each service's limits: they run one after another, Nominatim and Overpass get one request each, data.police.uk two, 100 ms apart, and PlanIt one, so don't run the script more than once a minute. A `429` is reported as a failure and not asked again. A free PlanWire key allows 25 requests a day, so each run uses one of them. There is no cache, so each run asks again.

The checks are `SMOKE_CHECKS` in `apps/api/src/smoke/smoke-checks.ts`. `smoke-checks.test.ts` runs them against the recordings the end-to-end tests use (`recordedFetch`), without reaching the network: every check passes as recorded; each source is skipped without its key or User-Agent and sent nothing; each fails, saying why, when it can't be reached, doesn't answer in time, gives an answer that no longer maps or has lost what it had, refuses a key, or answers `429`. It also checks that there is a check for every live Data Source in `apps/api/src/sources/`, every one whose answers are cached (the prepared ones never are), and for Anthropic's API, so a new adapter needs one ([Adding a Data Source adapter](developing.md#adding-a-data-source-adapter)).

## Benchmark

`pnpm bench` measures the performance targets of PRD §6.13, NFR-2.1 to NFR-2.5, on your machine and connection, against the live Data Sources. Like `pnpm smoke` it is opt-in, and never run in CI or by `pnpm test`, because it reaches the real services. It reads `.env`, as the API does, and like the API it won't run without `NOMINATIM_USER_AGENT`:

```bash
pnpm bench              # NG17 1AA's Search Area, uncached and cached, and Land Opportunities across Ashfield
pnpm bench --prepare    # runs pnpm data:prepare first, and times it: about 800 MB to download for Ashfield
pnpm bench --recorded   # answers every Data Source call from the recordings, to time our own code alone
```

It starts the API in its own process, as `pnpm dev` does, so its cache starts empty, on a free port, and asks it as the frontend does: `/api/geocode` for NG17 1AA, then, once that answers, NG17 1AA's 0.5 mile Search Area (`/api/area?…&sources=false`), each Layer (`/api/area/{layer}`), the scores and the Highlights in the General Scout Mode, all at once, in the frontend's order (`searchAreaRequests` in `apps/api/src/bench/bench-runs.ts`, after `loadArea` in `apps/web/src/useSearchArea.ts`). It asks over 6 connections, as a browser asks Vite's dev server over HTTP/1.1, so the rest wait for one to answer. It lists when each was answered, counted from the search, and how each Layer answered, then asks for it all again, from the cache. Then it finds Land Opportunities across the Land Coverage Area NG17 1AA is in (`/api/land?…&scope=land-coverage-area`), with FR-5.3's default filters, and again in order of capacity, as the Land panel asks when its order is changed. It judges each target:

| Target | Passes when |
|---|---|
| NFR-2.1 | Uncached, the last request is answered under 5 seconds after the search, with no Layer unavailable, as a Layer whose Data Source failed leaves the Search Area short of full. Planning Applications waiting for PlanIt's turn are said, and not waited for, as the page shows the rest meanwhile ([ADR 0003](../architectural-decision-record/0003-planning-applications-from-a-third-party-api.md)). |
| NFR-2.2 | Cached, the same requests are answered under 1 second. It says how many Data Source requests were sent again: only answers that were `ok` are cached, so a source that failed is asked again. |
| NFR-2.3 | The Search Area answered before the slowest Layer did, so the page drew the map, and each Layer that had come, while that one was on its way; or every Layer arrived within 100 ms of the first, leaving none to wait for. |
| NFR-2.4 | Both Land Opportunity searches answer under 3 seconds. Skipped while the land data of NG17 1AA's council, Ashfield, isn't prepared. |
| NFR-2.5 | With `--prepare`, `pnpm data:prepare` exits with `0` in under 15 minutes, having prepared one council. It refreshes every council prepared before, so a run that prepared several is timed but not judged. Skipped without `--prepare`. |

It exits with `1` if any target failed, and `0` otherwise, skipped ones included. A run on 9 October 2026, with data prepared for Ashfield, from a data centre that data.police.uk was answering `429 Too Many Requests` to (the API's own log of each failed call is left out):

## Survey of 20 Locations

`pnpm survey` is PRD §6.19's seventh success criterion made repeatable: it loads 20 real Locations, four each of town centre, suburb, village, rural edge and industrial, and writes down what each gave, so that a person can judge whether the app is useful and tune the scores against real Locations rather than recordings. What it found on 9 October 2026, and what was tuned because of it, are in [Phase 1 success checks](../phase-1-success-checks/README.md). Like `pnpm bench` it is opt-in, never run in CI or by `pnpm test`, reads `.env`, and won't run without `NOMINATIM_USER_AGENT`:

```bash
pnpm survey                          # writes docs/phase-1-success-checks/survey-of-20-locations.md
pnpm survey --json /tmp/survey.json  # and everything it found, as JSON, to analyse
pnpm survey --retry /tmp/survey.json # surveys again only the Locations that run didn't survey in full
pnpm survey --recorded               # answers every Data Source call from the recordings
```

The Locations are `SURVEY_LOCATIONS` in `apps/api/src/survey/locations.ts`, each found by its postcode: nine in Ashfield, so the Land Layer has land to show, and the rest across England's regions, from Camden Town to Threemilestone Industrial Estate outside Truro. It starts the API in its own process, as `pnpm bench` does, and surveys them one after another at the 0.5 mile Search Radius, so no Data Source is asked about two at once. For each, it searches for the postcode and asks for the Search Area, each Layer, the scores and the Highlights at once, as the frontend does, timing them as `pnpm bench` times NFR-2.1. While PlanIt's queue holds the Planning Applications it asks for the Planning Layer again every 5 seconds, as the frontend does, for up to two and a half minutes, so a run takes about 20 minutes. Then it asks for the scores and Highlights in every Scout Mode, all answered from the cache. Lastly, across each Land Coverage Area a Location is in, it finds the Possibly Unregistered Land Opportunities with FR-5.3's default filters and picks 10 spread from the largest to the smallest, for a person to check against HM Land Registry's index map (criterion 4).

It writes `survey-of-20-locations.md` (`--out` chooses another folder): each Location's local authority, how long it took, the Layers that weren't `ok`, its Land Opportunities; every Scout Mode's Location Score; each Category Score, and Competition in each Retail and Business mode; how each Category Score spread, from its least to its most, and how often it was 0 or 100, which is where tuning starts; how many Highlights each Scout Mode gave; each Location's General Highlights and the figures and points behind each Category Score; and the land to check, with a column for what the index map says. It judges nothing itself.

A Data Source that fails isn't cached, so asking each Scout Mode's scores would ask it again for each one, and data.police.uk's missing months could come back for one mode and not another. So where a Layer or the Planning Applications were unavailable, or data.police.uk didn't answer for some months, it asks only the General Scout Mode, and says so, naming the months. Run it again later with `--retry` and the JSON of the first run: it keeps the Locations the first run surveyed in full, and asks only about the rest. A run asks each Data Source about 20 Locations, as `pnpm bench` asks about one: about 37 requests a Location, 25 of them to data.police.uk, one to Overpass and one to PlanIt, and a few to postcodes.io, planning.data.gov.uk and Price Paid, so about 750 in all, and more where data.police.uk answers `429` and its months are asked again when the scores are. So run it rarely, and never twice in an hour.

`survey-runs.test.ts` runs it against the recordings over HTTP, without the network: NG17 1AA's Layers, its Location Score in every Scout Mode, as `scores.spec.ts` and `scout-modes.spec.ts` check them, its Category Scores, Competition by each mode's competitors and its seven Highlights; a postcode the recordings don't have, which is recorded as not found and asked nothing more; asking for the Planning Layer again while its Planning Applications are queued, then scoring them, and giving up at its limit; asking only General when a Layer is unavailable, or data.police.uk missed some months; `--retry` keeping a Location surveyed in full and asking it nothing; the sample of Possibly Unregistered land spread through a Land Coverage Area, asked once for each; and the report it writes, with FR-5.7's wording.
