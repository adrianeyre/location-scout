# Preparing land data

The optional `pnpm data:prepare` step for each Land Coverage Area. Back to the [README](../../README.md).

## Prepare land data (optional)

```bash
pnpm data:prepare                      # England-wide datasets + Ashfield land (default)
pnpm data:prepare --area "Mansfield"   # add more councils
pnpm data:prepare --all                # every English council: about 6.8 GB to download, hours
```

The app works without this step. Only the Land, Schools and Demographics Layers, and the scores that use them, are disabled, each saying to run `pnpm data:prepare`. Re-run it monthly to pick up new INSPIRE data: the app suggests it once a Land Coverage Area's INSPIRE Index Polygons are more than 2 months old.

So far it prepares the **council boundaries**, the **Demographics Layer's** four England-wide datasets, the **Schools Layer's** two, HM Land Registry's **CCOD and OCOD** when it has a key for them, and each **Land Coverage Area's land data**, **Land Parcels** and **postcodes**, and converts each with DuckDB's spatial extension into GeoParquet in the git-ignored `data/`.

- **Council boundaries**, from [ONS Open Geography](https://geoportal.statistics.gov.uk/): every council's boundary (Local Authority Districts, May 2026, generalised to 20 m and clipped to the coast: about 19 MB of GeoJSON), of which England's 296 are kept.
- **Output area boundaries**, from ONS Open Geography: England's 178,605 Census 2021 output areas (December 2021, generalised to 20 m and clipped to the coast), each with the LSOA and MSOA it is in from ONS's lookup. ONS gives them 2,000 a page, and the lookup 1,000, so this is about 270 requests, one after another, which take about six minutes.
- **Census 2021 by output area**, from [Nomis's bulk downloads](https://www.nomisweb.co.uk/sources/census_2021_bulk): each output area's usual residents (table TS001), households (TS041), residents per km² (TS006) and residents in each five-year age band (TS007A), about 11 MB of zipped CSV.
- **Household income estimates by MSOA**, from [ONS's small area income estimates](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/earningsandworkinghours/datasets/smallareaincomeestimatesformiddlelayersuperoutputareasenglandandwales) for the financial year ending March 2023: each MSOA's average total annual household income, from a 2 MB Excel workbook.
- **English Indices of Deprivation 2025**, from the [Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/english-indices-of-deprivation-2025): each LSOA's Index of Multiple Deprivation score, rank and decile, from File 7, a 10 MB CSV.
- **The schools register**, from the Department for Education's [Get Information about Schools](https://get-information-schools.service.gov.uk/Downloads) (GIAS): its "Establishment fields CSV", every establishment it has registered, of which the 24,382 schools open in England are kept, each with its phase, type, ages and point. GIAS publishes it afresh every day and hands it out only through its Downloads page, in three steps, as a browser does: the page's form asks for the file, sending back the cookies the page set; a page says when it is ready; and its form gives an 8 MB zip of a 65 MB CSV named for the day it was made, which is the dataset's date.
- **CCOD and OCOD**, HM Land Registry's [UK companies that own property in England and Wales](https://use-land-property-data.service.gov.uk/datasets/ccod) and [Overseas companies that own property in England and Wales](https://use-land-property-data.service.gov.uk/datasets/ocod), which the indicative **Ownership Categories** come from (PRD FR-5.4a, FR-6.0). They are free, but downloading them needs a [Use land and property data](https://use-land-property-data.service.gov.uk/) account, both datasets' licences agreed, and its API key as `LAND_REGISTRY_DATA_API_KEY` in `.env` ([Configure](getting-started.md#configure)), which `pnpm data:prepare` reads. Without the key it warns and skips them, and Land Parcels are only Possibly Unregistered or registered; if HM Land Registry refuses the key, or a download or a CSV fails, it warns, says why, and skips them too. With it, it asks [HM Land Registry's API](https://use-land-property-data.service.gov.uk/api-documentation) for each dataset's latest full file (`CCOD_FULL_2026_10.zip`), follows the link it gives, which is valid for 10 seconds, and reads the CSV inside the zip with GDAL. It keeps each title in England (its Region isn't Wales) with its title number, tenure, Property Address, District, County, Region, Postcode and first proprietor's Proprietorship Category, as `england/ccod.parquet` and `england/ocod.parquet`, but not its proprietors' names or addresses: an Ownership Category never names an owner. Its date is the day HM Land Registry's API says it was published. Each title's postcode is placed while matching with Code-Point Open (below), so if Code-Point Open fails they are skipped too. CCOD's full file is about 1 GB of CSV. HM Land Registry's licence limits how the addresses in them may be used (personal or non-commercial use, managing land, or preventing crime), and requires the statement "Information produced by HM Land Registry. © Crown copyright [year]" wherever they are used, which the app shows.
- **Code-Point Open**, from [Ordnance Survey](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/products/code-point-open) (14 MB, OGL): the middle of every postcode in Great Britain, 1,749,109 in its August 2026 release, which loads in about 4 seconds. Each Land Coverage Area keeps those within a mile of it (below), where the land sales near its Land Opportunities are placed (PRD FR-5.4b), and CCOD's and OCOD's titles are placed with it while they are matched. The OS Downloads API redirects to the latest release, whose month its link names, which is its date. If the download fails, `pnpm data:prepare` warns, says why, and prepares the rest: land sales then read as not prepared, and CCOD and OCOD are skipped.
- **The latest Ofsted inspections of state-funded schools**, from [Ofsted's monthly management information](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/monthly-management-information-ofsteds-school-inspections-outcomes): each school's latest report card, graded and ungraded inspections, a 17 MB CSV. Each month's is a new attachment of that page, so the latest is found through gov.uk's Content API, and the day it is "as at" is its date.

Each Land Coverage Area gets its own land data (PRD FR-6.1), in its own folder, and the Land Parcels the Land Opportunity Finder finds Land Opportunities among, built from it (FR-6.2):

- **INSPIRE Index Polygons**, from [HM Land Registry](https://use-land-property-data.service.gov.uk/datasets/inspire/download): the indicative outline of each registered freehold title in the council, as one GML file per local authority in a zip (Ashfield's is 10 MB, of a 64 MB GML of 58,066 polygons), with no API. HM Land Registry publishes them on the first Sunday of every month, and its download page says the day, which is their date. The page and the zips are handed out only to a request that sends back the cookies the page sets, as a browser does. HM Land Registry names councils its own way ("Ashfield District Council", "London Borough of Barnet", "Hull City Council"), so a council's file is the one whose name is the same as ONS's once both are tidied (`tidyName` in `inspire.ts`), or, for the eight whose names differ more, the one `INSPIRE_NAMES` gives for its ONS code: every one of England's 296 councils has its own. GDAL reads the GML inside the zip, told not to download its schema from HM Land Registry's own network, which can't be reached, and each polygon is turned from British National Grid into WGS 84. Its date and HM Land Registry's and Ordnance Survey's statements, with the year filled in and a link to the conditions, are stored with it in the manifest (FR-10.2).
- **OpenStreetMap's land use, buildings, roads and water**, from [Geofabrik](https://download.geofabrik.de/europe/united-kingdom/england.html)'s extracts. Geofabrik cuts its England extract (1.7 GB) into one per county, so a council is clipped from counties' extracts rather than England's (`chooseExtracts` in `geofabrik.ts`): first the county's whose outline covers most of it, then, while another's covers at least a hectare of what those chosen don't (`EXTRACT_MIN_GAIN_M2`), the one that covers most of it, each element once. Geofabrik's outlines follow county borders only roughly: they leave strips of Ashfield up to 130 m wide outside Nottinghamshire's, 63 ha of which Derbyshire's covers, so Ashfield is clipped from Nottinghamshire's and Derbyshire's extracts (39 and 44 MB). Of England's 296 councils, 231 are clipped from one county's extract, 38 from two and 27 from three or more: 9.6 km² of Wiltshire is outside Geofabrik's Wiltshire, so it takes seven. A few hectares between neighbouring counties' outlines are in neither, such as 1.7 ha of Ashfield on its border with Derbyshire, and what is wholly inside them is missing. Each `-latest` link redirects to a file named for the day its data dates from, `nottinghamshire-261008.osm.pbf`, which is its date. DuckDB's own `ST_ReadOSM` reads them, and the shapes are built in SQL (`loadOsm` in `geofabrik.ts`): a closed way with an area tag is a polygon, and a multipolygon relation is built from its member ways, its inner rings holes. A multipolygon mapped so that GEOS can build no area from it, as Hartshorne Wood's ways in Derbyshire are, is left out, and the run says how many were (one of the 3,182 in Derbyshire's and Nottinghamshire's extracts of 8 October 2026). GDAL reads `.osm.pbf` too, but it drops features from every layer but the one being read, so it isn't used.
- **Planning Constraints**, from [planning.data.gov.uk](https://www.planning.data.gov.uk/docs)'s bulk files: conservation areas, listed buildings and their outlines, Article 4 direction areas, tree preservation zones and flood risk zones, each as planning.data.gov.uk publishes it for all of England. The PRD names its bulk GeoJSON, but that is 3.4 GB for the six, 2.8 GB of it flood zones, which would take a typical line well past 15 minutes, so the same datasets' Parquet files are read instead, 613 MB, which DuckDB reads directly. Each is downloaded once a run, however many councils are prepared, and dated by the day planning.data.gov.uk last wrote it (its `Last-Modified`), or the day it was downloaded if it doesn't say. Constraints whose `end-date` has passed are left out, as the Planning Layer leaves them out.

- **Ownership Categories** (PRD FR-5.4a), with CCOD and OCOD. INSPIRE Index Polygons carry no title number, and CCOD and OCOD no outline, so each council's freehold titles (those whose District is the council's name, as `tidyName` tidies both) are matched to its INSPIRE Index Polygons by their Property Address (`matchCouncilTitles` in `title-ownership.ts`), with OQ-7's matching (`title-matching.ts`, [the spike](../../docs/spikes/oq-7-ccod-ocod-title-matching.md)). A title is matched only where its house number and street, at an OpenStreetMap address, or a building it names, as OpenStreetMap maps it, places it on exactly one polygon, within 2 km of its postcode (`TRUSTED_STEPS`). The spike found those matches right, or within 25 m, in every case it could check, but found them for only 5 to 7% of titles, and 1.9% of a council's land: land described by its street, and a postcode's point, place titles on the wrong polygon too often, so they match nothing. A polygon a council's or public body's title is matched to (Proprietorship Category "Local Authority", "County Council" or "Corporate Body", in CCOD) is **council or public-body owned**; one only companies' titles are matched to is **company-owned**; every other polygon is **other registered**, which says only that no company's or council's title was matched to it. A Land Parcel is **Possibly Unregistered**, or else in the category of the polygon that covers most of it, and lists the titles matched to the polygons that overlap it or are within 25 m of it (`titlesAroundMetres`). A council no title names as its District has no Ownership Categories, and the run warns. On the recordings, 3 of Ashfield's 5 made-up freehold titles are matched: Kings Mill Hospital names a building over two polygons, and land "on the north side of Lowmoor Road" names only a street.
- **Postcodes** (`postcodesNear` in `land-data.ts`): each of Code-Point Open's postcodes within 1 mile of the council's boundary (`NEARBY_LAND_SALES_RULES.withinMetres` in `packages/shared/src/land.ts`), across it too, as a point with its latitude and longitude beside it, for the land sales near its Land Opportunities (PRD FR-5.4b). Ashfield keeps 4,649, 2,750 of them inside it, which take 65 ms to find. It is dated, and credited as Ordnance Survey's licence requires, by Code-Point Open's release.
- **Land Parcels**, which `pnpm data:prepare` builds from the rest (`buildLandParcels` in `land-parcels.ts`, PRD FR-5.2, FR-5.4), in British National Grid, so in metres. OpenStreetMap's open or undeveloped land in the council (`OPEN_LAND`: `landuse` grass, meadow, brownfield, greenfield, farmland or construction, and `natural` scrub or grassland) is joined where it meets or overlaps and cut to the council's boundary. Then what covers it is taken away: buildings; roads, each widened to a width its kind is taken to have (`ROAD_HALF_WIDTHS`: 24 m for a motorway, 6 m for a residential street, 5 m for a service road); water, a river or canal mapped as a line 10 m wide and a stream 2 m; and parks, nature reserves, commons, gardens, village greens and recreation grounds (`PROTECTED_PARKS`). Footpaths, cycle paths, bridleways and tracks cross fields without dividing them, so they are left. Each contiguous piece of 100 m² or more that is left is a Land Parcel, numbered along a Hilbert curve (`E07000170-412`). Each is then tagged: the share of it INSPIRE Index Polygons cover, and the polygons that overlap it by 10 m² or more, so it is **Possibly Unregistered** if they cover less than half (`LAND_PARCEL_RULES` in `packages/shared/src/land.ts`); the Planning Constraints it intersects, beyond touching them, with the highest flood zone and whether it overlaps a listed building's outline; how far the nearest road is, up to 500 m; its area; and its estimated capacity, 30 homes a hectare after 40% for access and open space. Its outline is kept to within half a metre. Ashfield's 411 took 2.6 seconds to build on 9 October 2026, and make a 200 KB file. FR-5.2 also names gaps between building footprints, which aren't built yet: every garden and yard between houses would be one, so they need rules of their own.

OpenStreetMap's features and the Planning Constraints are kept whole if they reach within 100 m of the council's boundary (`CLIP_MARGIN_METRES` in `land-data.ts`), so a Land Parcel at its edge still finds the road, the buildings or the flood zone just across it (FR-5.3's 50 m from a road). Every land file has each shape's bounds beside it and is written along a Hilbert curve in row groups of 2,048, as the output areas are, so a query of a small area reads little of it. Each source's column names are checked first, and a council HM Land Registry has no file for, or no Geofabrik extract covers, stops the run, saying which, before anything is replaced.

The Census zips and the income workbook are read where they are, through DuckDB's spatial extension (GDAL reads a CSV inside a zip, and an Excel sheet), so nothing else is installed to read them. GIAS's and Ofsted's CSVs are in Windows-1252, which GDAL and DuckDB don't read, so they are decoded first (and GIAS's unzipped, `zip.ts`), and GIAS's British National Grid eastings and northings are turned into latitudes and longitudes with DuckDB. Each source's column names are checked first: if a new edition has changed them, `pnpm data:prepare` stops, naming what is missing, and changes nothing in `data/`. All of it, with Ashfield, took 10 minutes 25 seconds on 9 October 2026, nearly ten of them waiting on ONS's output area pages, and makes about 89 MB, 27 MB of it Ashfield's land data. Ashfield's land data alone, with its Land Parcels, took about a minute and a half: about 705 MB to download, 613 MB of it the Planning Constraints, which a 50 Mbit/s line takes about two minutes to fetch.

```text
data/
├── manifest.json                         # every file below, with its source and dates
├── england/                              # England-wide datasets
│   ├── council-boundaries.parquet
│   ├── output-area-boundaries.parquet    # 51 MB: each outline, its bounds, its LSOA and MSOA
│   ├── census-2021.parquet               # population, households, density and age bands
│   ├── income-estimates.parquet          # each MSOA's average household income
│   ├── indices-of-deprivation.parquet    # each LSOA's score, rank and decile
│   ├── schools.parquet                   # each open school's phase, type, ages and point
│   ├── ofsted-inspections.parquet        # each state-funded school's latest inspections
│   ├── ccod.parquet                      # with the key: UK companies' and councils' titles
│   └── ocod.parquet                      #   and overseas companies', without proprietors
└── ashfield/                             # each Land Coverage Area's own data
    ├── council-boundary.parquet
    ├── inspire-index-polygons.parquet    # each registered freehold title's outline
    ├── osm-land-use.parquet              # landuse, natural and leisure areas, with access
    ├── osm-buildings.parquet             # building areas, but building=no
    ├── osm-roads.parquet                 # highway ways as lines, footpaths too
    ├── osm-water.parquet                 # water areas, and waterways as lines
    ├── conservation-area.parquet         # each of planning.data.gov.uk's datasets,
    ├── listed-building.parquet           #   as it names it and with its columns,
    ├── listed-building-outline.parquet   #   with each shape (or point) as geometry
    ├── article-4-direction-area.parquet
    ├── tree-preservation-zone.parquet
    ├── flood-risk-zone.parquet
    ├── land-parcels.parquet              # each Land Parcel's outline, tags and Ownership Category
    └── postcodes.parquet                 # each postcode within a mile, from Code-Point Open
```

The first run makes Ashfield the one **Land Coverage Area**. `--area` adds a council, by name in any case (`--area "Mansfield"`) or by its ONS code (`--area E07000174`), and can be repeated for several: `--area "Mansfield" --area "Gedling"`. Every run refreshes the councils prepared before as well, so to drop one, delete `data/` and prepare again. A name ONS doesn't have is refused with the names it might mean ("Did you mean "Ashfield" or "Mansfield"?").

`--all` makes all 296 English councils Land Coverage Areas. On 9 October 2026 that was about 6.8 GB to download: 4.5 GB of INSPIRE zips (Cornwall's alone is 117 MB), 1.7 GB of Geofabrik's 47 county extracts, each downloaded once and kept until the run ends, and the same 613 MB of Planning Constraints, besides the England-wide datasets. It takes hours on a typical line, and the land data it makes is several GB. Councils are prepared an extract's worth at a time, so only those counties' features are in memory at once.

`data/manifest.json` lists each file with its source, where it was downloaded from, the date its data dates from (`sourceDate`: `2026-05` for the council boundaries, `2021-12` for the output areas, `2021-03-21`, Census day, for Census 2021, `2023-03` for the income estimates, `2025-10-30`, the day they were published, for the Indices of Deprivation, the day GIAS made its file for the schools register, such as `2026-10-09`, the day Ofsted's inspections are as at, such as `2026-08-31`, the day HM Land Registry published CCOD and OCOD, such as `2026-10-02`, and for each Land Coverage Area the month of Code-Point Open's release for its postcodes, such as `2026-08`, the day its INSPIRE Index Polygons were published, such as `2026-10-04`, the day of its Geofabrik extracts, such as `2026-10-08`, the oldest if they differ, which its Land Parcels, built from them, date from too, and the day planning.data.gov.uk wrote each of its datasets) and when it was prepared (`preparedAt`). Where a council's OpenStreetMap data comes from more than one extract, `sourceUrl` gives each, separated by a space. The INSPIRE Index Polygons', CCOD's and OCOD's entries also have the `attribution` HM Land Registry's conditions require, and the postcodes' Ordnance Survey's: their statements and the link to their conditions or licence. A re-run replaces every file and the manifest, keeping no history (PRD FR-6.5). Each file is written beside the one it replaces first, as `<file>.partial`, and only once all are ready do they replace the last run's, so a failed download or an unknown council changes nothing in `data/`.

DuckDB's spatial extension reads GeoJSON and reads and writes GeoParquet. `pnpm install` installs it after the packages (the `postinstall` of `scripts/data-prepare`): DuckDB downloads it once from extensions.duckdb.org into `~/.duckdb/extensions`. Offline, that only warns, and `pnpm data:prepare` installs it too. The backend and the tests only ever load it; without it, prepared data reads as not prepared.
