# Scores, Highlights and Scout Modes

How the Category Scores, Location Score, Highlights and Scout Modes work. Back to the [README](../../README.md).

## Scores

The Location Overview gives a **Location Score** and the **Category Scores** the chosen [Scout Mode](#scout-modes) weighs, each from 0 to 100 and each labelled "beta": eight in General, and Demand, Competition and Footfall proxy in the Retail and Business modes. Phase 1 has no regional averages to compare with, so they use fixed thresholds from PRD §6.4, tuned on 9 October 2026 against 20 real Locations ([Phase 1 success checks](../phase-1-success-checks/README.md#what-was-tuned-and-why)): Development activity, Property activity, the Retail and Business modes' Competition, and the change in median price's Highlights. Click a score, or press Enter on it, to see how it is reached: each input, the figure measured, the rule and thresholds used, and the points given. The scores come from `GET /api/scores`, which loads every Layer they need and scores them by the same rules every time. The Layers' Data Source calls are shared with the Layers' own requests and answered from the cache after, so scoring calls no source again. The scores arrive once the slowest of those Layers has, usually Crime. While PlanIt's queue holds the Planning Applications, Development activity, and Demand, which counts the residents they would bring, say "Loading planning applications (rate-limited)" and are left out of the Location Score until they come, and the frontend asks for the scores again every 5 seconds, as for the Planning Layer; the rest show meanwhile.

| Category Score | From | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Safety** | Crime, and Demographics if prepared | Crimes a year per 1,000 of the Search Area's estimated residents: 30 or fewer, 90 points, falling in proportion to 0 at 200 or more. Plus 10 points for a year-on-year fall of more than 10%, or minus 10 for a rise of more than 10%. |
| **Amenities** | Amenities | 70 points shared over the 13 types of amenity, for each type present, and 0.3 points an amenity, up to 100 of them. |
| **Schools** | Schools | 40 points for a primary school 800 m away or nearer, falling in proportion to 0 at 2.4 km, and 25 for a secondary school 2 km away or nearer, falling to 0 at 6 km; an all-through school counts as both. Plus up to 17.5 points, half of 35, for each one's latest Ofsted judgement: Outstanding 100%, Good 75%, Requires improvement 25%, Inadequate 0%; or, with no overall grade, the mean of the areas its inspection graded, a report card's Exceptional 100%, Strong standard 90%, Expected standard 75%, Needs attention 25% and Urgent improvement 0%. None if it found safeguarding wanting, or gave no grade. |
| **Transport** | Transport | 60 points for a railway station 800 m away or nearer, falling in proportion to 0 at 5 km, and 4 points a bus stop, up to 10. |
| **Green space** | Green space | 70 points for a park 300 m away or nearer, falling in proportion to 0 at 2 km, and 3 points a green space, up to 10. |
| **Property activity** | Property | 1.67 points a sale in the last 12 months, 100 at 60 sales or more (PRD §6.4 started at 100: postcodes.io's 100 postcodes rarely hold more than 60 a year). It measures how active the market is, not its quality. |
| **Development activity** | Planning's Planning Applications | A weighted count of the Planning Applications received in the Search Area in the last 3 years, each 1 and a major one 5: 0.4 points each, 100 at 250 or more (PRD §6.4 started at 100, which every town reached). It indicates change, not quality. |
| **Planning freedom** | Planning | 100, less a penalty for each type of Planning Constraint the Location is inside: flood zone 3 −40 or flood zone 2 −20 (only the higher counts), conservation area −20, listed building −20, Article 4 direction −15, tree preservation zone −5. |
| **Demand** | Demographics, and Planning's Planning Applications | The Search Area's estimated residents: 75 points at 20,000 or more, in proportion below. Plus up to 10 points as its MSOAs' average household income is above the England median, reaching 10 at 20% above, or minus up to 10 as it is below. Plus up to 15 points for the new residents the approved and pending Planning Applications would bring, estimated, reaching 15 at 1,000. Only the Retail and Business modes weigh it. |
| **Competition** | Amenities, and Demographics if prepared | The Scout Mode's competitors per 1,000 of the Search Area's estimated residents: none, 100 points, falling in proportion to 0 at 1 or more, or for a commoner business at the mode's own rate: shops 20, cafés, restaurants and hairdressers 3, takeaways 4 and convenience stores 2. Minus 10 if one is 100 m away or nearer. Fewer scores higher. Only the Retail and Business modes, which have competitors, weigh it. |
| **Footfall proxy** | Amenities and Transport | A weighted count of the shops, offices, bus stops and railway stations in the Search Area, where a shop, an office or a bus stop counts 1 and a railway station 10: 0 points for none, rising in proportion to 100 at 250 or more. Only the Retail and Business modes weigh it. |

Schools (PRD §6.4) is how near a primary and a secondary school are, and what Ofsted last made of them. Ofsted gave no overall grade from September 2024, and its report cards from November 2025 grade each area instead, so a school without one is scored by the mean of the areas it graded. The shares are starting points, to be tuned, in `SCORING_CONFIG.schools.ofsted`: a report card's expected standard is taken to be as good as a Good grade, needing attention as requiring improvement, and urgent improvement as inadequate. An ungraded inspection that names no grade, such as "Standards maintained", earns nothing, as there is no grade to go by. In NG17 1AA's 0.5 mile Search Area, Croft Primary School is 519 m away, 40 points, and its graded inspection of January 2025 gave no overall grade, but graded its five areas Good three times and Requires improvement twice, a mean of 55%, 9.6 points; Sutton Community Academy is 232 m away, 25 points, and its report card graded six areas at the expected standard and one as needing attention, 67.9%, 11.9 points. Schools is 87. Before `pnpm data:prepare`, it reads "Not prepared" and is left out of the Location Score.

Safety and Competition count per 1,000 of the residents the Demographics Layer ([Layers](layers-and-street-view.md#layers)) estimates in the Search Area (PRD §6.4). NG17 1AA's 0.5 mile Search Area had 2,024 crimes in the last 12 months for its estimated 9,052 residents, 223.6 per 1,000, so its Safety score is 0. If data.police.uk didn't answer for some of the last 12 months, the months it did answer for are scaled up to a year, and with no change to compare, the change gives no points.

Without an estimate of the residents, because `pnpm data:prepare` hasn't prepared the Demographics Layer, it is unavailable or the Search Area has no residents, Safety and Competition count per km² of the Search Area instead, taking 4,000 residents per km², a town's density (`residentsPerKm2` in the config). Safety's thresholds become 120 and 800 crimes per km², and Competition's 0 and 4 competitors per km², and the breakdown says so. Before `pnpm data:prepare`, NG17 1AA's 2,024 crimes over its 2.03 km² are 995 per km², and Safety is 0 all the same. The crime Highlights count crime as Safety does.

A competitor is an amenity whose OpenStreetMap tag, the one that made it its type in the Amenities Layer, is one of the Scout Mode's (PRD FR-4.3). So a café also tagged as a bakery is a café, and not one of the Retail mode's shops. Gyms, dental practices and nurseries are rarer than one per 1,000 residents, so Competition falls to 0 at 1 per 1,000, as PRD §6.4 gives it. Shops, cafés, restaurants, takeaways, hairdressers and convenience stores are commoner, and at 1 per 1,000 Competition was 0 wherever there was a high street, so each of those modes gives its own rate (`competitionPerThousand` in the mode's config), about twice what the towns surveyed have, so a typical high street scores about half ([why](../phase-1-success-checks/README.md#competition-each-common-business-has-its-own-rate)). In NG17 1AA's 0.5 mile Search Area there are 11 cafés for its 9,052 residents, 1.2 per 1,000, so the Business: Café mode's Competition is 59, falling to 0 at 3 per 1,000; the nearest is 110 m away. Its 2 gyms are 0.22 per 1,000, so Business: Gym's is 78. Its 5 restaurants are 0.55 per 1,000, 81.6 points, less 10 for the one 40 m away: 72. Counted per km² without the Demographics Layer, they would be 55, 75 and 70.

Demand (PRD §6.4) is how many people live in the Search Area to come in, and how much they have to spend. It needs the Demographics Layer: before `pnpm data:prepare`, Demand reads "Not prepared" and is left out of the Location Score. The Search Area's residents give up to 75 points, reaching 75 at 20,000. Its MSOAs' average household income, weighed by their households in it, is compared with the median of every English MSOA's in the same income estimates: 20% or more above it adds 10 points, 20% or more below takes 10, in proportion between. With no income estimate, income gives no points. The new residents the approved and pending Planning Applications in the Search Area would bring, estimated from the homes they propose ([Layers](layers-and-street-view.md#layers)), give up to 15 points, reaching 15 at 1,000 residents, about 420 homes, a starting point to be tuned (`plannedResidents` in the config). Without the Planning Applications, because PlanIt is unavailable, they give no points and the breakdown says why; while PlanIt's queue holds them, Demand waits for them. In NG17 1AA's 0.5 mile Search Area, 9,052 residents give 33.9 points, an average income of £38,890, 11.8% below the fixture's median of £44,091, takes 5.9, and the 120 residents the 50 homes approved or pending would bring give 1.8: Demand is 30. The fixture's income estimates are only the 7 MSOAs around Ashfield, so its median is theirs; prepared for all of England, it is England's.

Footfall proxy weighs what brings people past, from PRD §6.4: shops (any `shop=*`: other shops, convenience stores, supermarkets and hairdressers), offices, and transport stops. The PRD counts schools too, which Footfall proxy doesn't yet. NG17 1AA has 131 shops, 14 offices and 58 bus stops but no railway station, a weighted count of 203, so its Footfall proxy is 81. Footfall proxy needs both the Amenities and the Transport Layers: if either is unavailable, it is left out and the Overview names the Layer. A score never goes below 0 or above 100; if its points come to more or less, its breakdown says how far it was kept in.

Development activity (PRD §6.4) counts the Planning Applications received in the Search Area in the last 3 years, from PlanIt or PlanWire ([Layers](layers-and-street-view.md#layers)): each counts 1 towards a weighted count, and a major one 5, and each of the weighted count earns 0.4 points, 100 at 250 or more (`developmentActivity` in the config). PRD §6.4 started at 100, which every town and city of the 20 surveyed, and a Cotswold village, reached, so it couldn't tell a market town from London; at 250 a town scores about half. It says how busy the planning system is around the Location, not whether that is good. It goes by the Planning Applications, not the Planning Constraints, so it is scored when planning.data.gov.uk fails and PlanIt answers, and left out, "Unavailable", when PlanIt fails. If the provider has more than it gave, or may have, the breakdown says so. In NG17 1AA's 0.5 mile Search Area, PlanIt's 124 minor applications and 2 major ones make 134, so Development activity is 54. PlanIt has no recording for Sutton Parkway's Search Area, so there it is left out.

The Location Score is the weighted mean of the Category Scores, with the chosen [Scout Mode](#scout-modes)'s weights (PRD FR-4.2). A mode scores only the categories it gives a weight to. In General, the default: Safety 25, Amenities 15, Schools 10, Transport 15, Green space 10, Property activity 10, Development activity 5 and Planning freedom 10. Each category's share is its weight out of the weights counted. If a Layer is unavailable, its Category Score reads "Unavailable", it is left out of the Location Score, the other weights are renormalised to make up 100%, and the Overview says what was left out and why (PRD FR-3.4).

## Highlights

The Location Overview opens with **What you need to know**: the Search Area's Highlights, short 🔴 Concern, 🟠 Watch and 🟢 Good statements, Concerns first (PRD FR-3.7). Each comes from a fixed rule over one Layer's calculated figures, never from AI, and names that Layer. Choose one, by click or by keyboard, to focus its Layer: the Layer is switched on if it was off, its toggle in the Layers panel is pointed out, and the Overview moves to its statistics. The Highlights come from `GET /api/highlights`, which loads the same Layers as the scores, so it shares their Data Source calls and arrives with them. A Layer that is unavailable gives no Highlights, and the Overview says which Layers were left out.

Each [Scout Mode](#scout-modes) names the rules it applies, so the Overview gives the chosen mode's Highlights, and changes them when the mode changes; `/api/highlights?mode=homebuyer` gives that mode's.

Distances are given as the Overview gives them, to the nearest 10 m below 1 km. A change in median price needs 20 sales in each 12 months, so that a few flats or houses can't make one: with 10, as at first, Camden Town's 14 sales gave "down 51.8%" ([why](../phase-1-success-checks/README.md#highlights-a-change-in-median-price-needs-20-sales-in-each-12-months-not-10)). Rising prices are Good for an owner or an investor but not for a buyer, so the Homebuyer Scout Mode doesn't give them. In NG17 1AA's 0.5 mile Search Area the General Scout Mode gives seven: high crime (Concern); crime up 5.9% and the conservation area (Watch); all 13 types of amenity, 58 bus stops, a park 100 m away and the median price up 13.8% (Good). A Highlight for the homes Planning Applications would bring, such as PRD FR-3.7's "420 homes approved nearby", isn't built yet: the homes are estimated in the Overview, but no Highlight rule reads them.

## Scout Modes

The **Scout Mode** selector at the top answers one question at a time (PRD §6.5), and General is the default. A Scout Mode is config only, in `SCORING_CONFIG.modes` in `apps/api/src/scoring/config.ts`: its name and the question it answers, each Category Score's weight in the Location Score, its competitors' OpenStreetMap tags, the Layers it highlights, and the Highlight rules it applies. `GET /api/modes` lists them, and the selector offers whatever it lists. Choosing a mode loads the scores and Highlights again in it, with `?mode=`, but not the Search Area or its Layers, whose figures no mode changes. So each Category Score is the same in every mode that weighs it, but for Competition, which counts the mode's own competitors. The Location Score and its breakdown change, and the Scores say which mode they are in. The selector is kept as wide as "Development" so the header stays in one row at 1280 px; a longer name, such as "Business: Convenience store", is cut short there and given whole as the selector's tooltip.

Investment and Development highlight the Layers PRD FR-4.3a names. Investment highlights the Planning Layer, for its Planning Applications, and the Property Layer, for its sales and change in price. Development highlights the Planning Layer, for its Planning Constraints and Planning Applications, and the Land Layer, for its Land Opportunities. Each Layer a mode highlights is marked with its name, "★ Investment", in the Layers panel and over the Layer's statistics in the Location Overview, and Development's in the Land panel too.

The Retail and Business modes highlight the Amenities Layer, where their competitors are (PRD FR-4.3). On the map each competitor is ringed in purple and starred, and clustered only with other competitors, so an ordinary amenity's cluster never hides one. Its marker reads "Competitor: Costa, Café, 110 m from the Location", a cluster of them "3 competitors clustered", and its popup "★ Competitor in the Business: Café Scout Mode". The Amenities statistics in the Location Overview count them and name the nearest: "Competitors in the Business: Café Scout Mode (cafés): 11 in the Search Area, the nearest Costa, 110 m away." Switching the Amenities Layer off hides them with the rest.

### Weights of your own

Below the Location Score, **Weights** opens a slider for each category the Scout Mode weighs, giving its share of the Location Score to the whole per cent (PRD FR-4.4). They always make up 100%. They start at the mode's own weights, renormalised over the categories it weighs: in General, Safety 25%, Amenities and Transport 15% each, Schools, Green space, Property activity and Planning freedom 10% each, and Development activity 5%. Where they don't make up 100, the per cents left over after rounding down go to those rounded down most, the first first. Move one, by pointer or by arrow key, and the others share what is left in proportion to their weights before, or equally if they had none, each rounded to the whole per cent so that they still add up to 100%. Moving Safety to 40% in General leaves 60% to share: Amenities and Transport 12% each, Schools, Green space, Property activity and Planning freedom 8%, and Development activity 4%. In NG17 1AA's 0.5 mile Search Area that gives a Location Score of 49 rather than 62. A category moved to 0% counts for nothing, but keeps its slider.

The Location Score changes as the slider moves. The frontend weighs the Category Scores it already has by the same rule the backend weighs a mode's defaults by, `locationScore` in `packages/shared/src/location-score.ts`, so nothing loads again and no Data Source is called. The Category Scores never change, and the Location Score's breakdown says it is weighed "with your own weights for the General Scout Mode". **Reset to default** puts back the mode's weights from the config, and the Location Score the backend gave. Choosing another Scout Mode does the same, as each mode weighs its own categories.

The URL keeps your weights, so a reload or a shared link opens with them, and [Saved Locations](saved-locations-compare-and-report.md#saved-locations) keep them too: `&weights=safety:40,amenities:12,schools:8,transport:12,green-space:8,property-activity:8,development-activity:4,planning-freedom:8`. A slider changes them at every step, so the URL follows a quarter of a second after it stops, replacing its history entry rather than adding one a step. Weights in a URL that aren't whole per cents totalling 100%, or that don't name exactly the categories the mode weighs, are put back to the mode's defaults.

The backend describes its API at <http://localhost:8787/api/openapi.json> (OpenAPI 3.1). An interactive page at <http://localhost:8787/api/docs> lets you call each endpoint from the browser. The page loads Swagger UI from jsDelivr, so it needs an internet connection; the API itself doesn't.
